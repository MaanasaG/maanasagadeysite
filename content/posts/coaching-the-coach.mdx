### The mistake

## &ldquo;IC management, one level up&rdquo; is a lie.

You were good at managing engineers. Your report becomes a manager. You assume it&rsquo;s the same job with a bigger number on it. It isn&rsquo;t. It&rsquo;s a different job wearing the old job&rsquo;s clothes.

Managing an IC, you&rsquo;re coaching the work. You can see it: the bug, the design, the PR. You don&rsquo;t hand them answers, if you&rsquo;re any good you never did, but you can point at the thing and think out loud next to them.

Managing a manager, the work moved into a room you&rsquo;re not in. The 1:1 you&rsquo;ll never sit in. The call you hear about on Thursday. You can&rsquo;t point at the thing anymore, because you can&rsquo;t see the thing. You&rsquo;re coaching a person you can&rsquo;t watch.

That&rsquo;s the whole problem. It shows up in four places.

### The shift

## Four things change at once. All of them annoying.

**Distance.** The org dropped a whole person between you and the work, and your hands will itch to reach around them and touch it. Don&rsquo;t. Distance isn&rsquo;t a bug to patch, it&rsquo;s the assignment. You coach through them now, off the retelling, which means reading a room you&rsquo;re not in. Read the narrator, not the story: what they linger on, what they skip, whose name shows up with an edge, where they get defensive before you&rsquo;ve accused them of anything. Watch the wake, too. Does bad news reach you early or late? Who went quiet this month? Clock their distortion over time, so you know whether they over-blame themselves or always cast the same villain. You&rsquo;re not rebuilding the room. You&rsquo;re reading the person who was in it.

**Debrief.** Your feedback runs on a delay now. With an IC you catch things live. With a manager you get the story after the call is made, gift-wrapped, past tense. So the value moves to the debrief. You&rsquo;re not the seatbelt. You&rsquo;re the person who asks, afterward, &ldquo;what would you do again, and what would you set on fire?&rdquo;

**Depth.** The conversation gets less about tasks and more about humans. Half your 1:1s turn into them handing you their own people problems. The underperformer. The peer who won&rsquo;t cooperate. The promo they can&rsquo;t quite justify yet. So stop mining the 1:1 for status and give it back to them. It&rsquo;s management consulting now, one floor up, same pay.

**Demonstration.** Nobody puts this one in the job description. You&rsquo;re being copied. How you give credit, how you act when they&rsquo;re wrong, whether bad news is safe to bring you. They file all of it, and they&rsquo;ll run their team the way you run yours. Toddlers do the exact same thing. They ignore every instruction you give and then reproduce your worst habit at dinner. So the behavior you&rsquo;re careless about is the behavior you&rsquo;re teaching. It reaches people you&rsquo;ve never met. Act like it.

### The math

## Your impact stopped adding and started multiplying.

Coaching an IC is addition. You help one person do one thing better. Real, useful, bounded.

Coaching a manager is multiplication. Sharpen how they think and it doesn&rsquo;t stay put. It runs through everyone they manage, then everyone those people manage. Same hour of your time, completely different blast radius.

Which is exactly why it&rsquo;s impossible to put in a performance review. You can point to the shipped feature, the dead regression, the system that didn&rsquo;t fall over. You cannot point to &ldquo;asked a good question in a 1:1, and eighteen months later a team I&rsquo;ve never met runs clean.&rdquo; Your best work leaves no fingerprints. Nobody has fixed this. I haven&rsquo;t either.

### One last thing

## Put the answer down.

The whole shift on a sticky note: you went from watching the work to coaching the person who watches the work. From answers you could point at to questions you have to sit in.

It feels worse before it feels better. Answering is fast and makes you feel useful right now. Asking is slow, and half the time they land somewhere you wouldn&rsquo;t have. That&rsquo;s not the bug. That&rsquo;s the product. You&rsquo;re building judgment that runs when you&rsquo;re not in the room, because from here on, most of the rooms are rooms you&rsquo;re not in.

Put the answer down. Ask the question. Let them get there.
